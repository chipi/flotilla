# Two ideas (operator, 2026-11-19)

Pitched by the operator after a conversation with a friend. Decided as next steps after v2 is
rolled out (2026-11-19): plan both, the visual plane and mashup support, and first work out where
to draw the boundaries for this kind of project.

## The boundaries to decide first

- Visual plane: what it shows and to whom (operator only, the organization, anyone in the room);
  what it never shows; whether it lives in flotillad or beside it; live feed or periodic.
- Mashups: where flotilla stops and a plumbing tool starts (triggers, connectors,
  transformations); which outside triggers flotilla takes itself; whether knowledge (a store
  people ask) is a platform part or a fleet's; what "a business process" means as a unit (a fleet,
  a routine, a flow of routines).
- Both: what stays generic in the public platform and what is an example fleet.

## 1. A live view of flotilla, for a big screen

A page that shows what is going on live: how busy things are, and where. Beyond the operator's
dashboards, a view an organization could put on a second, big screen: a picture of the whole
flotilla at work, themed on flotilla's own world (fleets, routines, the sea).

First thoughts:

- The data exists already: runs starting and ending, cases moving, plans waiting for approval,
  spend, shadows and their pairs, the events stream, the metrics. The view needs no new data, only
  a way to watch it live (the server already renders pages; a small event stream would feed one).
- It has to be true. Every shape on the screen is a real run, case or approval, so the picture is
  also the trust message: what the agents are doing, visible to anyone walking past.
- Read-only, safe to leave on a wall: no controls, no case contents or secrets, a view-only access.
- Two layers: the operator's "now" (what needs me) stays the working page; the big-screen view is
  ambient (how the whole thing is moving).

## 2. flotilla as the workhorse of business-process mashups

Companies are wiring agents into how people work: an agent in Slack taking notes, knowledge bases
people can ask, small flows that tie tools together, a kind of "if this then that" with agents (n8n
and tools like it). Could flotilla be the building block for these? Routines are experts in a
domain and fleets bring them together, so: a company that wants its people to find and apply
knowledge, see who did what yesterday (the brief, in a team's context), and so on.

First thoughts:

- It fits what flotilla is for: agents as business processes that a business can trust. What the
  mashup tools lack is what flotilla has: a ledger of every decision, approvals for anything
  outward, autonomy earned per action, versions with shadows and rollback, and evaluation.
- Do not compete on connector count. Plumbing tools are good at triggers and connectors; flotilla
  could be the governed agent step they call, or call them as actions.
- Gaps to look at: triggers from outside (webhooks and chat events, not only schedules and
  monitors); a knowledge store (the knowledge plan in the installation's eval-data repo comes
  first); delivering results to people where they work (the brief and Slack already do some).
- Example fleets that would show it: a team brief (who did what yesterday, from the repo, the
  tracker and chat), a knowledge desk (ask the team's documents, answers with sources), meeting
  notes into decisions and follow-ups.
